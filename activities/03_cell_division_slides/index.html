Understanding Cell Division

The foundation of growth, reproduction, and repair

Cell division is the process by which a parent cell divides into two or more daughter cells. This fundamental biological process is essential for:

  • 🌱 Growth and development of organisms
  • 🔄 Repair and replacement of damaged cells
  • 👶 Reproduction in living organisms
  • 🧬 Transmission of genetic information
Learning Objectives: By the end of this presentation, you will understand the key differences between mitosis and meiosis, identify the phases of each process, and explain their biological significance.

The Cell Cycle

Interphase

G1, S, G2 phases

~90% of cycle

M Phase

Mitosis & Cytokinesis

~10% of cycle

Interphase is the period between cell divisions where the cell grows and replicates its DNA:

  • G1 Phase: Cell growth and normal functions
  • S Phase: DNA replication (synthesis)
  • G2 Phase: Further growth and preparation for division

Mitosis: Somatic Cell Division

Purpose

Mitosis produces two genetically identical daughter cells from a single parent cell.

Key Characteristics

  • Occurs in somatic (body) cells
  • Produces 2 daughter cells
  • Maintains chromosome number
  • Genetically identical to parent

Example

If a parent cell has 46 chromosomes (diploid), each daughter cell will also have 46 chromosomes.

2n → 2n + 2n

Phases of Mitosis: PMAT

Prophase

P
  • Chromatin condenses into chromosomes
  • Nuclear envelope breaks down
  • Spindle fibers form
  • Centrioles move to opposite poles

Metaphase

M
  • Chromosomes align at cell equator
  • Forms the metaphase plate
  • Spindle fibers attach to centromeres
  • Checkpoint ensures proper attachment

Anaphase

A
  • Sister chromatids separate
  • Move to opposite poles
  • Spindle fibers shorten
  • Cell begins to elongate

Telophase

T
  • Nuclear envelopes reform
  • Chromosomes decondense
  • Spindle fibers disappear
  • Cytokinesis begins

Meiosis: Reproductive Cell Division

Purpose

Meiosis produces four genetically unique gametes (sex cells) from a single parent cell.

Key Characteristics

  • Occurs in reproductive cells
  • Produces 4 daughter cells
  • Reduces chromosome number by half
  • Genetically unique (variation)
  • Two rounds of division (Meiosis I & II)

Example

If a parent cell has 46 chromosomes (diploid), each gamete will have 23 chromosomes (haploid).

2n → n + n + n + n

When fertilization occurs, two gametes combine to restore the diploid number.

Two Rounds of Division

Meiosis I

Reductional Division

  • Prophase I: Homologous chromosomes pair up (synapsis) and crossing over occurs
  • Metaphase I: Homologous pairs align at equator
  • Anaphase I: Homologous chromosomes separate
  • Telophase I: Two haploid cells form

Result: 2 haploid cells (each with duplicated chromosomes)

Meiosis II

Equational Division (like Mitosis)

  • Prophase II: Chromosomes condense again
  • Metaphase II: Chromosomes align at equator
  • Anaphase II: Sister chromatids separate
  • Telophase II: Four haploid cells form

Result: 4 haploid gametes (each with single chromosomes)

Mitosis vs. Meiosis: Key Differences

Feature Mitosis Meiosis
Purpose Growth, repair, asexual reproduction Sexual reproduction
Cell Type Somatic (body) cells Germ (reproductive) cells
Number of Divisions One Two (Meiosis I & II)
Daughter Cells Produced 2 4
Chromosome Number Same as parent (diploid → diploid) Half of parent (diploid → haploid)
Genetic Variation Identical to parent Genetically unique
Crossing Over No Yes (Prophase I)
